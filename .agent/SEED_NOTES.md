Voice-typing fixes: "eyebrows" = high price, "metal" = middle, "status" = statue, "noise" = nose,
"make you ask compiling" = make your closing question a compelling choice.

1. **Discount (negotiation):** When someone asks for a discount, never say yes or no. Say:
   "Okay, no problem — which part should we remove?"
2. **Three prices (pricing):** Never give a single price (it creates a yes/no reflex). Offer:
   Elite $20, Basic $2, Middle $10. You give a choice, not a yes/no decision; the middle is the one you want.
3. **Statue and the king (persuasion/communication):** An artist made a statue; the king said the nose was too big.
   The artist climbed up, threw some dust as if fixing the nose, came down and asked; the king said it's better now.
   Don't win the argument and lose the king — make them feel heard.
4. **Sell pain first (sales):** Don't start with specs or price. Order: the pain/problem → what this problem makes
   the customer lose ("if we changed this, what would happen to your business?") → the solution with budget and timeline.
   Make them feel the pain first.
5. **The 4-step sale (sales):** 1) find the problem/pain, 2) repeat the customer's words to emphasize it,
   3) what they lose if they don't buy (not what they gain), 4) make the close a choice:
   "Should we start this week or next?" (not "Does this work for you?").
6. **Attacked in a meeting (meetings):** Never defend yourself. Ask: "Show us what I'm missing."
   If he starts explaining basics, interrupt: "Let's skip the basics." Now he must either deliver the brilliant
   point he hinted at, or look foolish.
